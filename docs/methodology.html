How We Pick Products

A short page about where our data comes from, how we rank things, and the things we won't do even when they'd make more money.

What we actually do

For each category we collect the current product listings, then record four things: price, stated fitment, warranty and compliance claims, and the owner rating with its review count.

We read the low-rated reviews specifically, because that's where fitment problems and durability complaints surface. Every review on the listing is fetched with its star rating, date, the vehicle the owner drives and their own words, and the ones at three stars or below are kept verbatim. The star distribution is recorded too — an average of 4.5 can hide a 5.6% one-star rate, and that 5.6% is the part worth reading.

One thing about review counts that the listings don't tell you: a count is usually for the product line, not the exact part. The same intake is listed once per vehicle, and the "436 reviews" on the half-ton page includes owners of F-150s, Sierras and Colorados. So we match each review to the listing it was actually written against, and when a guide quotes a complaint we say which vehicle it came from. Where a pattern repeats across several vehicles and a large sample, we treat it as a property of the part; where it comes from one person, we say that instead.

We also check manufacturer documentation where it exists — the installation PDF, the warranty terms, the CARB exemption listing — because those are the claims a listing can get wrong in ways a review never catches. Where we have not checked one, the guide says that too: the intake guide carries a note that none of its three listings states an executive order number, and that an owner reported a smog failure, without either of us treating that as proof the part is illegal.

Where sources disagree, we say so rather than picking the more convenient one.

The three rankings

Every buying guide names three picks, each against a different criterion:

  • Best value — the cheapest option that isn't compromised in a way you'd regret. Not simply the lowest price.
  • Best design — judged on engineering and construction, not appearance alone, though appearance counts.
  • Best all-round — what we'd buy with our own money if we wanted to think about it once and be done.

Naming three picks forces a real decision. A list of ten "top" products ranked in order is a search-engine artefact, not advice.

What we won't do

  • We won't invent experience. We don't claim to have installed something we haven't. Where a claim comes from a forum thread or a video, we say that.
  • We won't publish a performance number we can't source. If no dyno data exists for a part, we say the data doesn't exist.
  • We won't backdate posts. Dates on this site are real publish dates. We'd rather look new than look established and be lying about it.
  • We won't hide the downside. Every pick gets a sentence about what you're giving up.

Corrections

If we got something wrong, we fix it and note the change. Send corrections to the address on the contact page. Prices and availability change constantly; we timestamp every price we quote so you can tell how stale it is.
