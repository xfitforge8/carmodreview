The short answer

The deciding dimension is not the rack and not the cover — it is the stake pocket. A rack that clamps into the stake pockets overlaps the rail a cover needs. A rack that mounts to the inner bed rail leaves those pockets alone, which is the only arrangement that reliably works.

The question people actually ask is whether a bed rack and a tonneau cover can live in the same bed. The products answer it in their own specification sheets, and they answer it in one word: the stake pocket. A rack that clamps into the stake pockets occupies real estate along the top of the bed rail. A cover needs that same strip to sit on. Put both there and one of them loses.

That is why the answer is not a brand and not a price. It is a measurement. Every figure below was read off a live Summit Racing product page on 17 November 2026, including the Notes field, where manufacturers write down the combination they know does not work.

The one rack that says it can share the bed

The Elevate Rack System at $849.99 is the clearest statement in the whole category, because the manufacturer wrote the rule into the listing itself. In the Notes field: “Works in conjunction with tonneau covers that mount inside bed rails without overlapping stake hole pockets.”

Read that carefully, because it contains both halves of the answer. It works, and the condition is that the cover mounts inside the bed rails and does not overlap the stake hole pockets. The rack does not care which brand of cover you bought — only where the cover puts its rails.

Elevate Rack System, bed rail mount → $849.99 · no reviews yet · checked 17 Nov 2026

Its other specifications are worth having in the same table. Mounting style is Bed rail, drilling required is No, material is Aluminum with a black powdercoated finish, and it is listed as a complete cargo rack system rather than a rack plus brackets. The telescoping design adjusts to 18 or 28 inches, so the crossbar height is a choice rather than a fixed number.

The second half of that same Notes field is where it gets specific: “Toyota Tacoma needs a tonneau cover with built-in T-slot rails to install this rack.” On a Tacoma the bed rail does not offer the flat inner lip other trucks do, so the cover itself has to bring the mounting channel. That is not a fault in either part. It is which surface the truck gives you.

And the third sentence is the one to plan around: covers without integrated T-slots need the Elevate TS Rail, sold separately. An adapter named in the product description and not listed alongside the rack is a part you have to go find. A search of Summit for that rail on the same date returned the rack itself and nothing else, so treat it as a call to the manufacturer, not a checkbox on the order page.

What the stake pocket costs you

Rough Country sells two racks that look like a family and behave like opposites. The 10204 at $399.99 mounts to the stake holes, requires no drilling, and is aluminum with a black powdercoated finish. The 10406 at $499.95 mounts to the bed rail and lists Drilling Required: Yes.

Rough Country 10204, stake-hole mount → $399.99 · no reviews yet · checked 17 Nov 2026

Rough Country 10406, bed rail mount → $499.95 · no reviews yet · checked 17 Nov 2026

The cheaper one is the one with the restriction you need to read. Its Notes field says: “Will not fit 6 ft. 7 in. bed. Will not fit tri-fold bed cover, or Rough Country’s hard shell rooftop tent.” Two exclusions in one sentence, and they are different kinds. The first is a bed length. The second is a cover type. A tri-fold cover folds in panels that stack forward over the bed rail — exactly the strip a stake-pocket rack has already claimed.

So the $399.99 rack is the one that clamps in without tools and the one that rules out the most common cover style in North America. The $499.95 sibling moves the mount to the bed rail, which is where the cover wants to be anyway, and pays for that with a drill.

Why “will it fit my truck” is the wrong first question

Most of the search traffic on this topic is fitment-shaped: a rack for a Tundra, an F-150, a Tacoma, a Gladiator, a Maverick. That is the question the listings are best at answering and the least interesting one, because the answer is usually yes. The interesting question is what the rack does to the top of the rail.

Access Tonneau’s Adarac line is the best illustration because it runs the same design across several trucks at several prices, and the prices are set by bed length rather than by capability. All three of these are aluminum, black powdercoated except where noted, mount to the stake holes, and require no drilling:

Adarac Pro, 6.00 ft. bed → $598.99 · silver powdercoated · checked 17 Nov 2026

Adarac Pro, Gladiator → $660.99 · no reviews yet · checked 17 Nov 2026

Adarac Pro, 8 ft. bed → $881.99 · no reviews yet · checked 17 Nov 2026

The specs separate them by one line each. The $598.99 part’s Notes reads “Fits 6.00 ft. bed models only.” The $881.99 part’s reads “Fits models with 8 ft. bed.” The Gladiator part carries no note at all, because on a Gladiator the bed length is not a variable — there is one. Three prices, one design, and the only thing that changes is how much aluminum is in the box.

Notice what is identical across all three: stake holes, no drilling. That is the pattern to watch for, and the reason the Adarac line appears so often in builds that also run a cover. The rack claims the pockets and stays out of the inner rail.

The adapter is a part, not a footnote

Here is where people lose a weekend. A rack is often not one item. BACKRACK sells its Safety Rack as a frame, and the thing that attaches that frame to your specific truck is a separate hardware kit with its own part number and its own price.

BACKRACK 30109 adapter → $139.99 · checked 17 Nov 2026

BACKRACK 30327 adapter, Tacoma → $139.99 · checked 17 Nov 2026

BACKRACK 30113 hardware kit → $139.99 · checked 17 Nov 2026

All three are $139.99, and the listings describe them in different language even though the price is the same. The 30109 is filed under “Bed and Ladder Rack Adapters.” The 30327 is filed under “Bed and Ladder Rack Accessories” with the product line “BACKRACK Adapter Bracket Hardware Kits” and the accessory type “Hardware kit”, sold as a kit. The 30113 sits in the same product line but its description is about a different job: “everything you need for mounting your Safety Rack to the frame itself.”

Frame mounting and bed mounting are not the same install. If the adapter kit you bought attaches to the frame, the rack is not sharing the bed rail with anything, and that changes the cover conversation in your favour. Check which of those two jobs a kit does before assuming the part numbers are interchangeable because the prices are.

The cheapest rack in the listing is not a rack

Search the category and a $94.95 Thule part comes up near the top, roughly a tenth of everything else here. It is a hardware kit, and its Notes field says exactly what for: “Mounting kit for Toyota Tacomas (without toolbox).” The price is real and the listing is real; the category placement is what misleads.

Thule 34000 mounting kit, Tacoma → $94.95 · checked 17 Nov 2026

Its Part Type is “Bed and Ladder Rack Accessories”, its product line is “Thule TracRac Bed Rack Accessories”, and its accessory type is “Hardware kit.” Every field is accurate and none of them says rack. That is the whole trap: correct metadata, wrong expectation.

The same trap exists in the other direction. A single-side accessory at $98.96 turned up in the same sweep — Rough Country’s 10632, steel rather than aluminum, mounting to the driver bed side and listing Drilling Required: Yes. Check Part Type before comparing any two numbers here: the spread between $94.95 and $881.99 is mostly a spread between kinds of object.

Reading the specification block in the right order

Every one of these listings carries the same fields, and four of them decide the cover question before you look at a photo.

Truck Bed Rack Mounting Style is the first. Bed rail and Stake holes are not two ways of saying the same thing; they are the two sides of this whole article. A bed rail mount leaves the pocket available. A stake hole mount uses it.

Drilling Required is the second. It is a yes or no on the listing and it tells you what the install costs in a way the price does not. The Rough Country pair makes the trade explicit: $399.99 with no drilling, or $499.95 with a drill. Neither is wrong. They are different afternoons.

Notes is the third, and it is the field almost nobody reads. It is where a manufacturer admits the combination that fails — “Will not fit tri-fold bed cover” is six words that save a return shipment. A listing with a populated Notes field is a listing where someone thought about the second owner.

Truck Bed Rack Style is the fourth, and it keeps you from comparing unlike things. Complete cargo rack system, Ladder rack, and Cargo rack describe different amounts of product. The Elevate at $849.99 is a complete system. The Rough Country at $399.99 is a ladder rack. The Adarac line is a cargo rack. All three hold cargo; they do not arrive as the same number of boxes.

Is any of this legal in California?

Yes, and this is the rare category where that is straightforward. A bed rack and a tonneau cover are cargo-carrying accessories. They do not touch the intake, the exhaust, the fuel system or the engine management, so there is no CARB Executive Order to look for and nothing here can fail a smog visual inspection.

The distinction matters because it is easy to assume anything bolted to a truck needs an EO number in California. Emissions parts do. A rack does not. If a listing in this category shows a California warning badge, read what it actually says — Proposition 65 warnings appear on all kinds of hardware for material content and have nothing to do with emissions compliance.

What a rack does change is the vehicle’s dimensions. Height above the cab and total loaded height are worth knowing before a parking structure, and that is a tape measure question rather than a legal one.

What to buy first

Decide the cover before the rack, because the cover’s mounting style is the constraint and the rack is the part with options. If the cover you want mounts inside the bed rails without overlapping the stake pockets, the Elevate at $849.99 says in writing that it will share the bed, and it does it without a drill. If the cover is a tri-fold, skip the $399.99 Rough Country — its own notes rule it out — and look at the $499.95 bed rail mount and the drill that comes with it.

If you have not chosen a cover yet, the $598.99 Adarac keeps the stake-pocket mount without drilling and leaves the inner rail alone, which keeps both doors open. And if the rack is going on a Tacoma, read the T-slot line before anything else, because that is the one truck in this sweep where the cover has to bring the mounting channel itself.
