# A 1.5-Inch Spacer or 1.75 Inches With Control Arms? What Each One Actually Changes

## EN

Buy the cheapest kit that gets your truck the height you measured, and put the
saved money into the alignment. Past about two inches, stop comparing spacers and
start comparing what the spacer does to the rest of the front end — at that point
the height is no longer the expensive part.

The four kits below run from $36.99 to $529.95 and carry 165 ratings between them.
The geometry does not change with the badge: any independent front-suspension
pickup behaves the same way when you move the knuckle relative to the frame.

### The four designs

A **lower strut spacer** sits between strut and lower control arm — cheapest,
nothing disassembled. An **upper strut spacer** sits inside the tower, which
ReadyLIFT's listing says reduces the risk of contacting the CV boot during
install. A **preloaded strut** brings a new spring and damper, so the ride changes
with the height. **Upper control arms** are the only one that fixes the geometry a
spacer breaks.

### Buy the rake you measured, not a round number

An owner of a 2019–2026 Silverado 1500 RST, under the 1.5-inch ReadyLIFT kit:

> <em>The visual rake measured closer to 1.5” and not the suggested 2” leveling
> kit, so I went with my gut. The ready lift 1.5” kit hit within 1/8” of even.</em>

Measure concrete to the top of each wheel arch and buy the difference — same
method on an F-150, a Ram or a Tundra. Go taller than your rake and you get a
nose-high truck: a Trail Boss owner on a 2-inch strut ordered a rear block
afterwards. Height is also the mechanism behind the warnings, because the upper
control arm follows the knuckle up and the alignment leaves the factory window.
Every listing here calls for one.

### Which one to buy

Standard Silverado 1500 and you measured 1.5 inches, take the MotoFab and put the
money into the alignment. Want the upper-mount design, read the ReadyLIFT 1.5-inch
exclusions first — no Super Cruise, no Trail Boss or AT4 suspension. Want the ride
to change and not just the stance, take the preloaded strut. Trail Boss, or going
past two inches, take the control arms: below that height they are insurance,
above it they are the part that keeps the front end serviceable.

### Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an alignment after a leveling kit?**

Yes, on every kit here. The listings say so, the reviews say so, and it is the one
step that turns a height change into a tyre-wear problem if you skip it.

**Will a leveling kit void my warranty?**

A spacer on its own generally does not, but a front-end failure after a suspension
modification will get looked at. In the US the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act puts the
burden on the manufacturer to show the modification caused the failure. Keep the
receipts.
