# Civic Si Bolt-Ons and Tune: What to Buy First, and What It Actually Gains

## EN

**The 1.5T in the Civic Si is the rare engine where the cheapest single purchase
out-gains every hard part combined — and the manufacturer of the tuning software
publishes the dyno sheet to prove it.** KTuner's own page for the 2017–2021 Si lists
a 23 PSI basemap at **+56 lb-ft of torque and +34 whp peak-to-peak**, with 35–40 whp
in the midrange. With their flex fuel kit on E35, that becomes **+75 lb-ft and
+65 whp**.

No cat-back exhaust in this sweep publishes a dyno figure at all. Not one. That is
not an accident of marketing — it is the honest state of the category, and it tells
you where to spend first.

### What this engine is

The Si has used a 1.5-litre turbocharged four since 2017, in two generations that
differ far less than the bodywork suggests. Both are rated on premium fuel.

| Generation | Years | Engine | EPA combined (manual) | City / highway |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10th gen Si | 2017–2020 | 1.5L turbo I4 | **32** (2017–2019), **30** (2020) | 28/38 (2017–19), 26/36 (2020) |
| 11th gen Si | 2022+ | 1.5L turbo I4 | **31** | 27/37 |

Those are the EPA's own per-model figures, and the 2020 drop is real: the EPA
lists the 2020 Si 4-door manual at 30 combined where the 2018 and 2019 cars are
32. If you are cross-shopping a 2020 against a 2019 and fuel cost matters, that
is a two-mpg difference on the same engine.

The practical point is that premium fuel is specified in every year, and every
KTuner map in the table below is written for 91 octane or better. Running a 23 PSI
map on 87 is not a shortcut; it is how you get knock.

### The tune, in the manufacturer's own numbers

These are KTuner's published claims for their own calibrations, read off their
site. They are not independent dyno results and this guide does not present them
as such — but they are the manufacturer's own, which makes them far more useful
than a vendor's rounded "up to +40 hp."

| Calibration | Claimed gain over factory | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 17–21, 23 PSI basemap | **+56 wtq, +34 whp peak**, 35–40 whp midrange | 91+ octane |
| 17–21, 23 PSI + flex fuel (E35) | **+75 wtq, +65 whp peak**, 60–65 whp midrange | Requires the flex fuel kit |
| 22+, Stage 1 (18 PSI Sport) | **+32 lb-ft, +30 whp** | Factory boost in Normal |
| 22+, Stage 2 (21 PSI) | **+52 lb-ft, +47 whp** | |
| 22+, Stage 2 + E30 | **+69 lb-ft, +63 whp** | Requires flex fuel |

Two things stand out. First, the 11th-gen car gains less on the same Stage 2 file —
**+47 whp versus +34 whp peak on the 10th**, but with a smaller torque number than
the older car's 23 PSI map. The newer ECU is more tightly controlled, and KTuner's
own numbers show it. Second, the ethanol jump is enormous on both: **E35 adds
roughly 30 whp on top of the pump-gas map**.

### What the tune actually changes, feature by feature

The interesting part of KTuner's feature list is not the power — it is how many
driveability complaints it addresses by name. From their published matrix for the
17–21 Si, every one of these is a final, shipping feature:

- **Rev hang disable.** The Si holds revs between shifts for emissions. Turning
  that off is the single most-praised change in owner reports, and it is a
  checkbox, not a tune.
- **Throttle dampening removal** — their words for the soft, delayed throttle the
  factory map has off the line.
- **Boost by gear**, so first and second can be capped while third and fourth get
  the full target.
- **Dual boost targets** split across Normal and Sport.
- **On-the-fly map switching**, so you can change calibrations without reflashing.
- **Real-time logging** of every sensor the ECU sees.

That list is why the tune is worth more than the exhaust on this car even setting
power aside. You are buying behaviour, not just a number.

### The order to buy things

**Tune first, always.** At $749 it is cheaper than the most-reviewed cat-back,
it is the only purchase on this page with a published dyno sheet, and it is the
one that fixes rev hang and throttle response. Nothing else on the list changes
how the car feels from the driver's seat as much.

**Downpipe second, if you want more.** The catted downpipe is the highest-value
hard part on a turbo car because it is the restriction closest to the turbine.
$599.99 gets you the 16–21 piece with 85 reviews behind it. This is also the part
that starts the emissions conversation — see below.

**Cat-back third, and buy it for sound.** With 209 reviews the MAP sedan system
is the best-evidenced exhaust in this whole sweep of the catalog. Buy it because
you want to hear the car. Do not buy it expecting the dyno sheet that does not
exist.

**Front pipe last.** At $349.99 it is the cheapest pipe in the table and the
least consequential. It is a supporting part for a downpipe, not a first purchase.

**Flex fuel only if you have E85 nearby.** It is the single biggest gain on the
page — E35 turns the 17–21 car's +34 whp into +65 — but it is worthless without
a station you can actually reach, and it locks you to the pump.

### What owners actually complain about

The review counts point at something a spec sheet cannot. **The 17–21 Si has the
thickest aftermarket evidence of any compact in this sweep** — 209 reviews on the
sedan cat-back, 85 on the catted downpipe, 46 on the front pipe — and that depth
means the failure modes are already documented rather than waiting to be discovered.

The pattern across those listings is fitment, not function. Coupe and sedan are
different systems with different part numbers, and the listings say so in capitals
for a reason: MAP sells `HDAX-CBE-SN` for the sedan and `HDAX-CBE-CPE` for the
coupe, and buying the wrong one is the most common mistake. The 209-review system
and the 171-review coupe system are not interchangeable.

The 22+ car is the opposite situation. Its cat-back carries 48 reviews, its
downpipe 17, and KTuner's own feature matrix lists fewer things as final on the
newer ECU. If you own an 11th-gen Si, you are earlier in the curve and the
aftermarket has less to tell you yet.

### What the tune does not fix

Worth saying plainly, because a $749 flash gets oversold: it does not address the
oil dilution issue that the 1.5T has been discussed for since 2017, it does not
change the clutch, and it does not make the car a Type R. A 23 PSI basemap on a
car with 200,000 miles on its original clutch will find that clutch quickly. If
you are near a clutch replacement, that is the purchase to make first.

It also does not carry a warranty. KTuner's own site is explicit that the software
is for off-road testing and that the vehicle should be flashed back to factory
before driving on a public road — that sentence is there for a reason, and it is
the sentence a dealer will point at.


### The CARB question, which on this car is the whole question

None of the parts above state a CARB Executive Order number on their listings.
Neither does KTuner's own product page or its Si application page — the site
carries a prominent notice that the software is "for off-road research and
development testing only" and that every vehicle should be flashed back to factory
before being driven on a public road.

So the honest summary, and it is not a comfortable one: **without an EO number,
a downpipe and a tune are not legal for street use in California and will fail a
smog visual inspection.** For the cat-back the position is different — a cat-back
sits behind the converter and does not touch emissions equipment — but no listing
here states its status either way, and silence is not clearance. Ask the seller
for the EO number before you buy, and if they cannot produce one, assume there
isn't one.

If you are in a state that inspects, the tune is the part to think hardest about,
because a flashed ECU is not something you can quietly leave in place at test time.
KTuner's own documentation says to flash back to stock — which is also the answer
to "can I undo this," and it is yes.

## ES

**El 1.5 turbo del Civic Si es el motor donde la compra más barata supera a todas las
piezas físicas juntas — y el fabricante del software publica la hoja de dyno que lo
demuestra.** La página de KTuner para el Si 2017–2021 declara **+56 lb-ft y +34 whp**
con un mapa base de 23 PSI, y **+75 lb-ft y +65 whp** con E35.

Ningún cat-back de este barrido publica una cifra de dyno. Ni uno. No es casualidad: es
el estado honesto de la categoría, e indica dónde gastar primero.

| Generación | Años | EPA combinado (manual) |

### El orden de compra

**1. El tune primero.** A $749 es más barato que el cat-back con más reseñas ($999.99),
es la única pieza de la página con hoja de dyno publicada, y elimina el rev hang.

**2. El downpipe después.** El catted de 16–21 a $599.99 con 85 reseñas es la pieza
física de mayor valor en un turbo.

**3. El cat-back por sonido.** El MAP sedán a $999.99 con **209 reseñas** es el escape
mejor documentado del catálogo. No lo compres esperando la hoja de dyno que no existe.

**4. El front pipe al final.** $349.99, la pieza menos decisiva.

**5. Flex fuel solo con E85 cerca.** El mayor incremento, pero inútil sin una estación
a la que puedas llegar.

### California

Ninguna de estas piezas declara un número de Executive Order de CARB, y KTuner indica
que su software es "for off-road research and development testing only". **Sin EO, un
downpipe y un tune no son legales para uso en calle en California y fallan la
inspección visual de smog.** El cat-back está detrás del convertidor y es distinto, pero
ningún listado declara su estado. Pide el número de EO antes de comprar.
