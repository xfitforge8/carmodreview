# Honda Accord 2.0T Exhaust and Tune: The Order That Actually Works

## EN

**The Accord 2.0T is the best engine Honda put in a family sedan, and the
aftermarket for it is priced like a sports car.** A cat-back runs $1,019.99 to
$1,495.00. A flash runs $549 to $749. Both are real purchases, and the order you
make them in decides which one you actually feel.

The temptation is to buy the exhaust first, because it is the part you can hear.
That is the wrong way round on this platform, and the reason is in the
manufacturer's own dyno numbers.

### What this engine is

The 2.0T is a detuned Civic Type R engine: 2.0 litres, turbocharged, and in the
Accord it was sold as the upgrade over the 1.5T. Through the 10th generation it
came with a 10-speed automatic, or a 6-speed manual in the Sport trim.

EPA figures from fueleconomy.gov, read 18 November 2026:

| Year | Transmission | Combined | City | Highway |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 10-speed automatic | **27** | 23 | 34 |
| 2021 | 10-speed automatic | **26** | 22 | 32 |
| 2022 | 10-speed automatic (Sport/Touring) | **26** | 22 | 32 |
| 2020 | 6-speed manual | **26** | 22 | 32 |

**And then it stopped.** The 11th-generation Accord, from 2023, offers a 1.5T and
a 2.0-litre hybrid. There is no 2.0T in it. That matters for shopping, and it is
the single most common way people get this wrong, so it is worth stating plainly:
a listing that advertises "2022+ Honda Accord 2.0T" is describing a car that
was never built. The 2.0T is a **2018–2022** part.

### The tune is the bigger number

Here is the finding this article is built on. KTuner publishes its own results
for the 18–22 Accord 2.0T, read on their site on 18 November 2026, and they are
specific:

| Tune | Torque gain | Peak power gain | Peak-to-peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | up to **50 lb-ft** | up to **30 whp** | 16 whp |
| Stage 2 | up to **88 lb-ft** | up to **60 whp** | 27 whp |

Those are the manufacturer's numbers, not an independent dyno, and they should be
read as such. But even discounted heavily they describe a change you will feel,
and they cost **$549** (KTunerFlash V1.2) or **$749** (V2 Touch with the
all-in-one display), both read off MAPerformance listings on 18 November 2026.

A cat-back, by contrast, is behind the turbo. On a turbocharged engine the
downpipe is the restriction, not the pipe after it. That is the same point the
GTI guide makes, and it applies here unchanged: **spend on the tune first.** The
exhaust is what you buy when you want the car to sound like what it now is.

Two things about that table are worth saying out loud.
**The spread is $475 for the same job.** Every one of these replaces the same
section of pipe on the same car. The difference is material, muffler design, tip
finish and brand, not the amount of pipe you get.

**Review counts are almost meaningless here.** The highest count on any of the
five is one. That is not because the parts are bad; it is because a family sedan
exhaust is a small market compared with a truck or a GTI, and because
MAPerformance pools ratings across fitments. Do not read "1 review" as a warning
and do not read a high pooled count as evidence about your car.

### The cheap parts that are not the exhaust

Three smaller parts came up repeatedly on this platform and they are the ones
that actually unlock the tune:

- **PRL front pipe upgrade, $429.00.** 3-inch mandrel-bent, and PRL's own
  description says it is *"recommended for customers who have already purchased a
  downpipe upgrade."* It is the piece after the downpipe, not a substitute for it.
- **PRL intercooler charge pipe kit, $429.00.** One review.
- **PRL flex fuel kit, $579.99.** This is what makes the E30 maps in the KTuner
  tune usable — the manufacturer states all included tunes support E30 *"via flex
  fuel kit installation."* No kit, no E30.

### What the tune actually changes, part by part

The headline numbers above are the sales pitch. The feature list on KTuner's page
is the more useful document, because it says what the flash reaches, and several
of those things are worth more to a daily driver than 60 whp.

**Dual boost targets on the ECO button.** This is the standout for a family
sedan. The tune keeps factory boost targets in ECO mode and puts the Stage 2
targets in Normal and Sport. You get a quiet, economical car for the commute and
the full tune when you press the button, from the same flash, with no second
purchase. Almost nothing else on this list gives you a switch.

**Boost by gear.** Boost is limited per gear rather than delivered all at once.
On a front-wheel-drive car with this much torque that is a traction feature as
much as a tuning one, and it is the reason a Stage 2 car does not simply spin
through first and second.

**Throttle dampening removed at low end.** The factory softens the first part of
the pedal travel for smoothness. KTuner lists this as *"Removed throttle dampening
on low end, better performance off the line."* This is the change most people
describe as "it feels awake," and it costs no power at all.

**Rev hang disabled on the manual.** If you have the 6-speed, this is the single
most-recommended part of the package. The factory holds the revs up between
shifts; KTuner lists it as *"Rev Hang Disable (MT Only)."* Removing it makes the
manual feel like a manual. It does nothing on the automatic, so read the list for
your own transmission.

**Real-time logging and flash back to stock at any time.** Both matter for the
warranty conversation and neither is a performance feature. Being able to return
the car to factory is what makes this reversible, and reversibility is the whole
argument for a flash over a piggyback on a car you still owe money on.

### What none of this changes

**The tune and the exhaust do not touch emissions equipment.** A cat-back starts
behind the catalytic converter, so the cat stays. A flash reprograms the factory
computer. Neither removes a converter.

**KTuner's own product page is explicit about intended use.** It states the unit
is *"for off-road research and development testing only. Every vehicle should be
flashed back to factory before being driven on a public road."* That is the
manufacturer's language, not this site's framing, and it belongs in front of
anyone deciding whether to buy one.

**No CARB Executive Order number was found for KTuner on the Accord 2.0T.** Their
site does not publish one, the applications page does not list one, and the
MAPerformance listing carries no CARB text either. For a part that reprograms
fuel and ignition, that means in California it is **not legal for street use**,
and a smog inspection is the point where that becomes a practical problem rather
than a theoretical one. If a seller tells you otherwise, ask for the EO number on
the box.

**The cat-backs are a different question, and none of the five listings states
CARB status either way.** None of the five product pages carried CARB or
Executive Order text on 18 November 2026 — they were searched for it. That is
silence, not clearance. On a California car, get the answer from the seller in
writing before you buy, and do not assume a cat-back is automatically exempt
just because the converter stays put.

### The order to buy things

1. **The tune.** $549 or $749, and it is the only purchase on this list that
   changes what the car does rather than what it sounds like.
2. **Data logging.** Bought with the tune. Do not skip it; the whole point of a
   flash is that you can see what the engine is doing afterward.
3. **The cat-back.** $1,019.99 at the floor. Buy it for sound, because that is
   what it is.
4. **The front pipe and flex fuel parts, only if you are going further.** The
   front pipe assumes a downpipe. The flex fuel kit assumes you want E30.

If you only do one thing, do the tune, and put the exhaust on the list for later.
The car will be quicker on the day you flash it, and it will sound like a family
sedan until you do not.

## ES

**El Accord 2.0T es el mejor motor que Honda puso en un sedán familiar, y su
aftermarket está tasado como el de un deportivo.** Un cat-back cuesta entre
$1,019.99 y $1,495.00. Un flash cuesta entre $549 y $749. Ambos son compras
reales, y **el orden en que los hagas decide cuál vas a sentir.**

### Qué es este motor

El 2.0T es un motor de Civic Type R despotenciado. Hasta la décima generación se
ofreció con automático de 10 velocidades o manual de 6. Cifras de la EPA leídas
en fueleconomy.gov el 18 de noviembre de 2026:

| Año | Transmisión | Combinado | Ciudad | Carretera |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | automático 10 vel. | **27** | 23 | 34 |
| 2021 | automático 10 vel. | **26** | 22 | 32 |
| 2022 | automático 10 vel. (Sport/Touring) | **26** | 22 | 32 |

**Y ahí terminó.** El Accord de onceava generación, desde 2023, ofrece un 1.5T y
un 2.0 híbrido. No hay 2.0T. El 2.0T es una pieza de **2018–2022**, y un anuncio
que diga "2022+ Accord 2.0T" describe un coche que nunca existió.

### El tuneo es la cifra mayor

Números publicados por KTuner para el Accord 2.0T 18–22, leídos el 18 de
noviembre de 2026:

| Tune | Ganancia de par | Ganancia de potencia |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | hasta **50 lb-ft** | hasta **30 whp** |
| Stage 2 | hasta **88 lb-ft** | hasta **60 whp** |

Son cifras del fabricante, no de un banco independiente, y deben leerse así. Aun
descontándolas mucho describen un cambio que se siente, y cuestan **$549** o
**$749**, leídos en anuncios de MAPerformance el 18 de noviembre de 2026.

Un cat-back está detrás del turbo. En un motor turbo la restricción es el
downpipe, no el tubo posterior. **Compra primero el tuneo.** El escape es lo que
compras cuando quieres que el coche suene a lo que ahora es.

### Los cinco cat-back, con precio

Leídos en páginas de producto de MAPerformance el 18 de noviembre de 2026.

| Sistema | Precio | Reseñas |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal R&D | **$1,019.99** | 1 |
| MagnaFlow (19393) | **$1,231.00** | — |
| DC Sports | **$1,330.00** | — |
| Borla S-Type (140786) | **$1,338.99** | 1 |
| Remark Sports Touring | **$1,495.00** | — |

Hay **$475** de diferencia para el mismo trabajo. Y **el número de reseñas casi no

aplicaciones.

### Lo que nada de esto cambia

**El cat-back y el flash no tocan el equipo de emisiones.** El cat-back empieza
detrás del catalizador, así que el catalizador se queda.

**La propia página de KTuner declara el uso previsto:** *"for off-road research
and development testing only"*, y que el vehículo debe volver a fábrica antes de
circular por vía pública. Eso es lenguaje del fabricante, no de este sitio.

**No se encontró un número de Orden Ejecutiva CARB para KTuner en el Accord
2.0T.** Ni su web lo publica, ni la página de aplicaciones lo lista, ni el anuncio
de MAPerformance trae texto CARB. Para una pieza que reescribe combustible y
encendido, eso significa que en California **no es legal para uso en calle** y
falla la inspección de smog. Si un vendedor dice lo contrario, pide el número EO
de la caja.

### El orden de compra

1. **El tuneo** — $549 o $749. La única compra que cambia lo que el coche hace.
2. **El registro de datos** — va con el tuneo.
3. **El cat-back** — desde $1,019.99. Se compra por sonido.
4. **Front pipe y flex fuel** — solo si sigues adelante.

Si solo haces una cosa, haz el tuneo.
