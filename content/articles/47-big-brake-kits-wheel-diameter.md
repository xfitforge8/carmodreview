# Big Brake Kits: The Wheel Diameter Field Decides Before the Price

## EN

**Four truck big brake kits sit between $1,010.99 and $3,268.50, and the number
that rules out three of them is not the price — it is the minimum wheel diameter
printed near the bottom of each spec block.** A kit that does not clear your
wheels is not a cheaper option or a more expensive one. It is not an option.

### The wheel diameter field is the real filter

This is the field most buyers meet last, and it should be first.

A 20-inch minimum means a 20-inch wheel, not a 20-inch tire. If your truck came
with 18s, a kit requiring 20s means buying wheels - and given the prices here,
that can be most of the kit's cost again. The field is doing you a favour by
appearing on the listing at all.

Note the pattern in the table: the two most expensive kits both require 20 in.
wheels, and the least expensive named one requires 18. On a truck that is
already sitting on 20s, the cheapest-to-fit kit is not the cheapest kit.

**One listing publishes no minimum at all** — the PowerStop kit at $1,269.84.
That is not a claim it fits everything. It means the field is blank, and a blank
field is something to ask about rather than assume.

### "Big brake kit" does not mean many pistons

PowerStop sells these under one big-brake banner at two prices, and the piston
count differs between them. Its spec row reads **Front Caliper Piston Quantity:
One** on the Jeep application at $1,010.99 and **Two** on the Chevy/GMC kit at
$1,269.84.

One piston per caliper is a sliding caliper — the same basic arrangement as a
base economy car. Two is better. Neither is six.

That matters because piston count is the thing most shoppers believe they are
buying when they pay for a big brake kit, and on the cheaper kits they are not
buying it. The Wilwood kits here all read **Six**, forged billet aluminum.

If piston count is what you want, check the row. It is printed.

### The rear rotor that is bigger than the front

A fifth part worth reading alongside these is PowerStop's **BBK-JK-002R**, the
Jeep application at $1,010.99. It publishes both rotor diameters, and they invert
what the name suggests:

- **Front: 13.50 in.**
- **Rear: 14.25 in.**

The rear disc is larger than the front one on a kit sold as a front-and-rear big
brake upgrade. That is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly, and it is on
the listing for anyone who looks.

The same part number also carries the **one-piston** front caliper noted above,
so the two most surprising fields on this shelf belong to the same kit.

For comparison, the stock F-150 front rotors documented in our rotor guide run
12.12 in. to 13.78 in. depending on year — so the PowerStop front disc is in
roughly stock territory, while the Wilwood 16.00 in. rotor is not close.

### Remanufactured: Yes, on a four-figure part

Two of the kits here list **Remanufactured: Yes** — the PowerStop at $1,269.84
and its Jeep sibling at $1,010.99. Both Wilwood kits list **No**.

Remanufactured calipers are common and legitimate; they are rebuilt to spec and
sold with a warranty. But it is a meaningful difference in what arrives in the
box, and at a four-figure price it belongs in the decision rather than in fine
print.

The field is in the spec block, a few rows above the price.

### Bolt pattern separates the classes before price does

There is a second hard filter in the same spec block, and on a truck it usually
settles the question before the wheel diameter does.

- **PowerStop KC2073-36:** `8 x 6.50 in.` / `8 x 165.10mm`
- **Wilwood AERO6 140-9789-D:** `6 x 5.50 in.` / `6 x 139.70mm`
- **Wilwood TX6R 140-13877 and 140-13873:** `8 x 6.50 in.` / `8 x 165.10mm`

Six-lug and eight-lug are not interchangeable, and they map onto vehicle classes
rather than preferences. The AERO6 is the only six-lug kit here; the other three
are all eight-lug 6.50 in.

If your truck is eight-lug, the $2,692.54 AERO6 is not on your list at any
price, and the field that removes it sits above the rotor diameter. If it is
six-lug, the reverse is true, and the three cheaper-to-fit eight-lug options are
all unavailable to you.

This is why the spec block is worth reading in order rather than shopping by
price: two hard filters can eliminate a kit before you have considered anything
you might have an opinion about.

### What the expensive kits actually include

Reading down the "Rear Rotor Included" column explains much of the price gap:

- **PowerStop:** Rear Rotor Included: **Yes**. A complete front-and-rear set.
- **All three Wilwood kits:** Rear Rotor Included: **No**.

The Wilwood kits are **front only**. So the $3,200.56 and $3,268.50 figures buy
one axle, and the $1,269.84 PowerStop figure buys two. Compared per axle, the gap
between them is far wider than the sticker prices suggest.

The two TX6R kits also differ from each other by $68 for a **15.00 in.** rotor
against a **16.00 in.** one, both six-piston, both 20-inch minimum. If you are
already committed to 20-inch wheels, that is a small premium for an inch of
diameter; if you are not, both are out.

### What the hardware does not include

Three fields read the same across every kit in the table:

- **Master Cylinder Included: No**
- **Brake Booster Included: No**
- **Front Spindles Included: No**

The Wilwood kits are described as volume-matched to work with the OE power brake
system, which is the manufacturer's claim about compatibility rather than a
statement that nothing else needs attention. Braided stainless flex lines are
noted as a separate purchase for some applications.

The AERO6 kit carries an additional note worth reading twice: it **will not fit
vehicles with aluminum spindles**, only cast iron. On a truck where spindle
material varies by year and trim, that is a fitment question to settle before
ordering, not after.

### Does a big brake kit need a CARB Executive Order?

No. Brakes are not emissions equipment. There is no CARB EO number to look for
on a caliper, rotor or brake line, and no part in this article affects a
California smog inspection.

That is worth stating because the question comes up whenever a California buyer
sees a four-figure truck part. The answer here is simply that the emissions
rules do not reach it.

### What I could not verify

- **Stopping distance improvement for any of these kits.** No listing here
  publishes a measured 60-0 or 70-0 figure, before or after. The case for a kit
  is heat capacity and fade resistance, and this article makes no shorter-stop
  claim.
- **Whether the PowerStop kits fit a specific truck.** I read spec blocks, not
  application tables. With no published minimum wheel diameter on the Chevy/GMC
  kit, fitment is a question for the seller by part number.
- **What the Wilwood kits weigh** against stock hardware, which is the other half
  of the unsprung-mass question.
- **Rotor thickness at discard** for these kits, so the resurfacing arithmetic in
  our rotor guide could be applied to them.
- **Whether the 16.00 in. rotor clears a specific 20-inch wheel.** Minimum wheel
  diameter is a diameter, not a spoke profile, and caliper clearance varies by
  wheel design.

### El diámetro de rueda es el filtro real

Un mínimo de 20 pulgadas se refiere a la rueda, no al neumático. Si su camioneta
trae 18, un kit que exige 20 significa comprar ruedas, y a estos precios eso puede
costar casi tanto como el kit.

Los dos kits más caros exigen 20 pulgadas; el más barato con dato publicado pide
18. **El kit PowerStop de $1.269,84 no publica mínimo alguno**: eso no significa
que sirva para todo, significa que el campo está vacío.

### «Big brake» no significa muchos pistones

El kit PowerStop declara **un pistón** por pinza en su versión Jeep y **dos** en la
versión Chevy/GMC. Uno es una pinza deslizante, la misma disposición de un auto
económico. Los Wilwood declaran **seis** pistones en aluminio forjado.

### El disco trasero más grande que el delantero

En la versión Jeep del PowerStop, el disco **delantero mide 13,50 in.** y el
**trasero 14,25 in.** En un kit vendido como mejora delantera y trasera, eso es
inusual y está impreso en la ficha. Como referencia, los discos delanteros de
fábrica de la F-150 documentados en nuestra guía van de 12,12 a 13,78 in.

### Remanufacturado y contenido

Los dos PowerStop indican **Remanufacturado: Sí**; los Wilwood, **No**. Solo el
PowerStop incluye disco trasero, así que $3.268,50 compra un eje y $1.269,84 compra
dos. Ningún kit incluye cilindro maestro, servofreno ni mangueras.

El kit AERO6 no sirve para vehículos con manguetas de aluminio, solo de hierro
fundido: una pregunta de montaje que hay que resolver antes de pedir.

### California

Los frenos no son equipo de emisiones. No hay Executive Order de CARB que buscar y
no afectan a la inspección de humos.

### Lo que no pude verificar

No se publica ninguna distancia de frenado medida, ni el peso de los kits, ni el
grosor de descarte de estos discos. Leí fichas de especificaciones, no tablas de
aplicación: confirme el montaje por número de parte.
