# Intake Options From $65 to $476: What You Get at Each Price

## EN

Intakes are sold as one product category but they are really five different
purchases at five different prices, and the differences between them are not
proportional to what you pay. This article walks the whole range in order of
price, using current listings for the 2019–2026 Silverado 1500, and says what
each one changes and what it does not.

Every price and review count below was read off the live listing on 4 November
2026. No horsepower figure appears anywhere on this page, and the section at the
bottom explains why.

### The one thing that decides whether you notice a difference

Before price, there is a more useful question: **where does the filter draw
air from?**

An intake that pulls from inside the engine bay is pulling the hottest air
available once the truck is warm and stationary. An intake that pulls from the
factory fender location, behind the headlight, is pulling outside air. That
single design detail separates a real cold air intake from a shiny pipe with a
cone on the end, and it cuts across price: the cheapest option on this page
keeps the factory cold-air path, and a $190 open cone does not.

So the price ladder below is not a quality ladder. It is a list of five
different engineering trades.

### 1. The $65–97 drop-in panel filter

**What it is:** a reusable filter that drops into the factory airbox, reusing
every other part of the factory intake.

**What it changes:** the filter medium, and only that. Airbox volume, intake
tract diameter, and the cold-air draw location all stay exactly as the
engineers designed them.

**What it does not change:** anything you can hear. This is the option people
skip because it is boring, and it is the one that carries the least risk.

**Why it is first on this list:** it keeps the factory cold-air path by
definition, because it keeps the factory airbox. There is no sealed-versus-open
question to get wrong and no MAF contamination risk from a poorly oiled cone —
though an oiled panel filter still needs correct oiling, and that risk does not
disappear, it only shrinks with the smaller filter area.

### 2. The $190 budget full intake

**What it is:** a complete replacement intake — tube, filter, and whatever
shielding the maker includes — at roughly a third of what the established brands
charge.

**What it changes:** the sound, mainly. A cone or open element removes the
airbox's sound damping, which is most of what people are buying when they buy an
intake.

**What it does not change:** the power, on a stock tune, for the reason set out
in the companion guide on this site: the ECU measures incoming air and fuels it
accordingly, so extra flow capacity does not become extra commanded power.

**Where the money went:** at this price the answer is usually the housing.
A sealed box is an expensive part to tool and mould. A bent heat shield and an
open element is not. Whether the listing you are looking at is sealed or shielded
is the thing to check at this price point, and the listings are not always
forthright about it.

### 3. The $399–436 closed-box band, oiled or dry

**What it is:** the mainstream aftermarket answer. In this band the makers who
build a genuine closed housing — Corsa and aFe both say "closed box" in the
product name — sell a sealed unit that draws from the factory fender location,
a large reusable filter, and a moulded intake tube.

**What it changes:** the sound, the induction noise under load, and the filter
service interval. It keeps the cold-air draw location, so it does not undo the
one thing the factory got right.

**What it does not change:** the power, still, on a stock tune. This is the
price band where marketing language is loudest about horsepower, so it is worth
repeating: without a tune, an intake is a sound and serviceability purchase.

The oiled-versus-dry decision matters more here than at the drop-in level,
because the filter is bigger and takes more oil when it is recharged. Dry media
costs about the same in this band and removes the failure mode outright.

### 4. The $476 top of the range

**What it is:** the most expensive intake in the mainstream catalogue, and at
this price the extra money goes into the filtration element rather than the
housing.

The Donaldson PowerCore filter is a different technology from cotton-gauze: a
pleated, high-density media that is designed to hold more contaminant in the same
volume. That is the honest argument for the price — not more airflow, but better
filtration at similar flow, which is the trade that actually matters on a truck
seeing dust.

### The DIY option, and what it actually is

There is a fourth path people ask about: modify the factory airbox yourself.
The usual versions are cutting the resonator or silencer off the airbox, or
drilling the lower half.

**What it gets you:** more induction noise, cheaply, because you have removed the
part whose job was to reduce it.

**What it costs you:** the factory airbox is a tuned volume. Cutting into it
changes the resonance the engineers designed around, and it can also change
where the air is drawn from — the modification most likely to make a truck
slower in traffic is the one that opens the box to engine-bay air.

**The honest summary:** a resonator delete is a sound modification with the same
outcome as a $190 intake, done for the price of a hacksaw, with no ability to
put it back. If that trade appeals to you it is not irrational — but it is a
one-way change to a part that costs more to replace than the cheap intake does to
buy, and it is worth knowing that going in.

**The one DIY job genuinely worth doing:** check and clean your MAF sensor, and
replace the factory paper panel filter on schedule. Both are cheap, both protect
the engine, and neither has a downside.

### Which one to buy

Written plainly, because the honest answer depends on what you want:

- **You want it to work and cost little:** the $97 dry drop-in. It keeps the
  factory cold-air path and carries no oiling risk.
- **You want the sound, cheaply:** a sealed-box intake in the $399 band, dry
  media, correct fitment for your engine.
- **You drive in dust or on gravel:** pay for filtration, which means the
  PowerCore at the top, and price the replacement element into the decision.
- **You want power:** buy the intake and the tune together, or buy neither. On a
  stock tune the intake is not a power modification, at any price on this page.
- **You want to spend nothing:** clean the MAF and keep the paper filter fresh.

## What we could not verify

**No horsepower claim is made on this page.** None of the listings examined
carried a dyno chart, and we ran no test of our own on any of these parts. A
horsepower number without a chart is marketing, and repeating one would put a
figure on this page that we cannot stand behind.

**Sealed-versus-open construction is reported from the listings, not from
inspection.** We did not open any of these boxes. Where a listing states a sealed
or closed housing, this page says so; where it does not state one, this page does
not claim one either.

**No listing stated a CARB executive order number.** Absence of a claim is not
proof of illegality, but if your state inspects, confirm exemption with the
manufacturer before ordering rather than assuming it either way.

**Review counts are reported instead of star ratings,** because star ratings
render inconsistently across these pages. A high review count means more people
can tell you whether it bolted up cleanly; it does not mean the part is better.

**Prices are list prices read on 4 November 2026** and will move. Fitment notes
are as the listings state them, and several of these listings are specific to one
engine — check yours before ordering.

### Lo único que decide si notas la diferencia

Antes del precio hay una pregunta más útil: **¿de dónde aspira el aire el filtro?**

Una admisión que toma aire de dentro del vano motor está tomando el aire más
caliente disponible cuando el camión está caliente y parado. Una admisión que
toma del sitio de fábrica en el guardabarros, detrás del faro, toma aire
exterior. Ese único detalle separa una admisión de aire frío real de un tubo
brillante con un cono en la punta, y no depende del precio: la opción más barata
de esta página conserva la ruta de aire frío de fábrica, y un cono abierto de
$190 no.

Así que la escala de precios de abajo no es una escala de calidad. Es una lista
de cinco intercambios de ingeniería distintos.

**Qué es:** un filtro reutilizable que entra en la caja de aire de fábrica,
reutilizando todas las demás piezas de la admisión original.

**Qué cambia:** el material filtrante, y solo eso. El volumen de la caja, el
diámetro del conducto y el punto de toma de aire frío quedan exactamente como
los diseñaron los ingenieros.

**Qué no cambia:** nada que puedas oír. Es la opción que la gente salta por
aburrida, y es la que menos riesgo tiene.

porque conserva la caja de fábrica. No hay una decisión de sellado contra abierto

### La opción DIY, y qué es realmente

Hay un cuarto camino que la gente pregunta: modificar la caja de aire de fábrica
uno mismo. Las versiones habituales son cortar el resonador o silenciador de la
caja, o perforar la mitad inferior.

**Qué te da:** más ruido de admisión, barato, porque has quitado la pieza cuyo
trabajo era reducirlo.

**Qué te cuesta:** la caja de fábrica es un volumen afinado. Cortarla cambia la
resonancia que los ingenieros diseñaron, y también puede cambiar de dónde toma el
aire — la modificación con más probabilidad de hacer un camión más lento en el
tráfico es la que abre la caja al aire del vano motor.

**El resumen honesto:** eliminar el resonador es una modificación de sonido con
el mismo resultado que una admisión de $190, hecha por el precio de una sierra,
y sin capacidad de volver atrás. Si ese intercambio te atrae no es irracional,
pero es un cambio de un solo sentido en una pieza que cuesta más reemplazar que
lo que cuesta comprar la admisión barata, y vale la pena saberlo de antemano.

**El único trabajo DIY que de verdad vale la pena:** revisar y limpiar tu sensor
MAF, y cambiar el filtro de panel de papel de fábrica a su debido tiempo. Ambos
son baratos, ambos protegen el motor, y ninguno tiene desventaja.
