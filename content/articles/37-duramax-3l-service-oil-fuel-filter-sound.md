# 3.0L Duramax Service: The Oil Is a Licence, the Filter Is a Part Number

## EN

**The 3.0L Duramax asks two questions at service time, and they are not the same
kind of question.** The oil is a licensing question: GM publishes a list of bottles
it has tested, and a bottle that merely says it is suitable is not on it. The fuel
filter is a part-number question: the same engine was built with two different
housings, and the two GM part numbers are not interchangeable. Get either wrong and
the truck still runs, which is what makes both mistakes easy to keep making.

Every price and review count below was read on a live Summit Racing listing on
17 November 2026, and every fitment claim comes from that listing's applications
page.

### The oil is a licence, not a brand

GM runs a separate approval for diesel oils called **dexos D** at
`gmdexos.com/brands/dexosd/index.html`. It is not a marketing page — it carries a
supplier and an eleven-digit licence number for each entry, and GM's about page
says unlicensed products *"are not approved or recommended for use in GM
vehicles"* and that using one *"could cause engine damage that may not be covered
under warranty."*

Two details matter more than any forum thread. **dexos D is licensed in both 0W-20
and 5W-30**, so "what weight does a 3.0 Duramax take" depends on the truck, not the
engine code — the filler cap settles it. And dexos D *"meet[s] or exceed[s] the
performance of GM dexos 2 engine oils"*, so a diesel that ran dexos 2 moves over at
its next change.

Mobil 1 ESP X2 appears on GM's licensed list twice, as `DD40BAED816` and
`DD40BCCB015`, both 0W-20. It is **$11.99** a quart at Summit with a single
five-star review. ACDelco's own diesel dexos D is also **$11.99** a quart and carries

listed as **ACDELCO LIGHT DUTY DIESEL** under `DD0751IA015`. Both bottles are
licensed products, which is the point: the licence is what you are paying for, and
the price is the same either way.

### Why "suitable for" is the wording to slow down on

Here is the part of this that costs people money. Schaeffer's sells an
Advanced Light-Duty Diesel 0W-20 at **$15.32** a quart, and its Summit
description says the oil *"is specifically formulated and suitable for use in
vehicles that require General Motors dexos D."* That sentence reads like approval.
It is not on GM's licensed-brand list — no Schaeffer's entry appears anywhere on
the dexos D page as read on 17 November 2026.

That is not an accusation about the oil's quality. It is a statement about what
"suitable" does in a product description versus what an eleven-digit number on the
back label does. GM spells out the check: *"look for the dexos® icon on the front
label and the 11 digit alphanumeric dexos® license number on the back label. Unless
an oil package displays these two markings, the engine oil is not an authentic,
licensed dexos® product."*

Liqui Moly's Molygen 0W-20 fails the same test: **$23.99**, one five-star review, no
Liqui Moly entry anywhere on GM's list either. Both are good oils and neither is
licensed, and the 3.0 Duramax is the engine where that distinction carries a
warranty.

### The fuel filter is two different parts

This is the mistake made on a Saturday afternoon. ACDelco sells two genuine GM
fuel filters that both fit the 3.0L Duramax, and they are different components:

**13539108** is a **cartridge element**, **$46.99** with **4.75 stars across 6
reviews**. Its spec sheet reads `Fuel Filter Style: Cartridge`, element included,
and no bracket, fittings or clamps — you keep the housing and replace the inside.

**13543066** is a **complete assembly**, **$71.99** with no reviews. Its specs read
`Style: Stock`, plastic housing, **quick-connect on both inlet and outlet**, and
`Mounting Bracket Included: Yes` — you replace the housing, not its contents.

### Reading a fitment page properly

The applications page is the most useful thing on a parts site and the most
misread. It gives a **Results Found** count then rows naming make, model, years,
engine size and engine family. Read **Engine Family**, not the count.

WIX's WF10451 proves it. Its page returns **676 applications**, 2-micron synthetic
fibre, 4.16 inches long, no bracket or fittings, **$51.99**, no reviews. The count
says nothing; the rows say everything: `CHEVROLET SILVERADO 1500 | 2020-2022 |
3.0L/183 | DURAMAX L6 DIESEL`, and again for 2023-2025 and 2026.

### Is any of this legal in California?

The oil and fuel filters on this page are service parts and carry no emissions
question at all. The exhaust parts do.

Gibson's own application notes for the 615640 read **"50 State Legal / California
Emissions Compliant"** — the clearest statement in this cluster, and the reason it
is the one to reach for in a state that inspects.

PPE's resonator delete is listed as an air-intake part, and neither its listing nor
its application notes carry a CARB Executive Order number. That is not a statement
that it is illegal; it is a statement that **no EO number appears, so nothing on the
listing supports it as California-legal** — which is the question a visual smog
inspection asks.

aFe's Vulcan listing carries the standard red-flag line instead: *"It is illegal to
install parts or components that bypass, defeat, or render inoperative the
manufacturers' technology and design necessary to reduce vehicle emissions […]"* A
particulate-filter-back system on a diesel is what that sentence is written about.

Underneath it all is
[Cal. Veh. Code § 27156](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=27156):
no emissions-control device may be removed, altered or defeated. A part with an EO
number has answered that. A part without one leaves it to you.

### What to buy first

Read the oil filler cap, check that weight against GM's dexos D list, then buy the
bottle whose back label carries the eleven-digit licence number. For the fuel
filter, look at the housing before ordering — cartridge at **$46.99** or complete
assembly at **$71.99**. For the oil filter, the extra three dollars on the ACDelco
at **$12.99** over the WIX at **$9.99** buys a fitment page that names the engine
family and specs listing both a bypass valve and an anti-drainback valve. And if
the goal is the noise rather than the service, the resonator delete at **$116.99**
is a bolt-on with no cutting and no tuning.

### ¿Es legal en California?

Los aceites y filtros de esta página son piezas de servicio y no plantean cuestión
de emisiones. Las de escape sí.

Las notas de aplicación del Gibson 615640 dicen **"50 State Legal / California
Emissions Compliant"**, la afirmación más clara del grupo y la razón para elegirlo en
un estado que inspecciona.

El eliminador de resonador de PPE figura como pieza de admisión y ni su listado ni
sus notas llevan número de Orden Ejecutiva CARB. No significa que sea ilegal:
significa que **al no aparecer ningún EO, nada en el listado lo respalda como legal
en California**, y una inspección visual de smog es donde eso pesa.

El listado del aFe Vulcan lleva la advertencia habitual: *"Es ilegal instalar piezas
o componentes que anulen, desactiven o inutilicen la tecnología y el diseño del
fabricante necesarios para reducir las emisiones […]"* Un sistema posterior al
filtro de partículas en un diésel es exactamente la pieza sobre la que se escribe esa
frase.

La norma de fondo es el
[Cal. Veh. Code § 27156](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=27156):
ningún dispositivo de control de emisiones puede retirarse, alterarse ni anularse.
Una pieza con número de EO ya respondió la pregunta; una sin él se la deja al
comprador.
