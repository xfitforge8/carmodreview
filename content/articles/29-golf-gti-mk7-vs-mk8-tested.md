# Golf GTI: MK7 vs MK8 vs MK8.5, Generation by Generation

## EN

**The Golf GTI's headline numbers barely moved across three generations, and two
of the published test scorecards say the newest car is the slowest of the three
to 60 — while the same magazine's track test says it is the fastest.** Both
statements are true at once, and the gap between them is the most useful thing
to understand before you buy a used GTI or budget a tune.

Every figure below was read from Car and Driver's own instrumented tests on
16 November 2026, plus Volkswagen's and APR's published data. Nothing here is a
manufacturer estimate relabelled as a measurement.

### The three cars, tested

| | 2015 MK7 | 2022 MK8 | 2025 MK8.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power | 220 hp @ 4700 | 241 hp @ 6500 | 241 hp @ 6500 |
| Torque | 258 lb-ft @ 1500 | 273 lb-ft @ 1600 | 273 lb-ft @ 1600 |
| Gearbox | 6-speed manual | 7-speed DCT | 7-speed DCT |
| Curb weight | **3105 lb** | 3154 lb | **3293 lb** |
| 0–60 mph | 5.8 sec | **5.1 sec** | 5.6 sec |
| ¼-mile | 14.4 @ 100 | **13.6 @ 105** | 14.2 @ 100 |
| Braking, 70–0 | 163 ft | **151 ft** | 158 ft |
| Skidpad | 0.91 g | **0.97 g** | 0.91 g |
| Top speed | 124 mph (gov) | 155 mph (mfr) | 129 mph (gov) |

Read down the columns and the shape of the story is obvious: the MK8 was a
genuine step up, and the MK8.5 gave a chunk of it back. Against the 2022 car,
the refreshed 2025 is **half a second slower to 60**, **seven feet longer
stopping from 70**, and **0.06 g down on the skidpad** — with identical power,
identical torque, and identical brake hardware at 13.4 inches front and 12.2
inches rear. It also gained **139 lb**.

Two honest caveats, because they matter and nobody prints them.

The 2015 test predates the rollout convention Car and Driver uses now. Both the
2022 and 2025 figures state that results *"omit 1-ft rollout of 0.3 sec"*; the
2015 sheet carries no such note, so its 5.8 seconds may not be measured to the
same standard as the other two. Treat the MK7 row as indicative and the MK8/MK8.5
comparison as like-for-like.

And the 2015 car was a **Performance package** example — 220 hp rather than the
standard 210, with the larger brakes and the limited-slip differential. The
standard MK7 was slower than the number in that table, not faster.

### The track result disagrees with the drag strip

Here is where it stops being a simple regression story.

Car and Driver runs the same car at Virginia International Raceway every year in
Lightning Lap, and that test is far longer and more revealing than a 0–60 pull.
The two MK8 cars:

| | 2022 MK8 | 2025 MK8.5 |
|---|---|---|
| Lap time | 3:12.1 | **3:11.3** |
| Weight as run | 3311 lb | 3285 lb |
| Tires | Hankook Ventus S1 evo3 | Hankook Ventus S1 evo3 |

**The 2025 car was eight tenths quicker around VIR than the 2022 car**, on the
same tire model, while recording a worse skidpad and a longer stop from 70.

That combination is not a contradiction so much as a lesson about what these
tests measure. The skidpad is one steady-state circle, and the 70–0 stop is one
number from one surface on one day. A full lap averages four miles of braking,
turn-in and corner exit, where a car with better body control and a more
cooperative gearbox can win by eight tenths while losing a single-number test.

It also tells you the two generations are **close enough that a single test day
can decide the result**. If you are choosing between a 2022 and a 2025 on the
basis of a tenth, you are choosing on noise. Note too that the Lightning Lap
weights do not match the road-test curb weights in either direction — 3293 lb in
the road test versus 3285 lb at VIR for the same model year is a difference of
option load and fuel, which is why the lap time is the more comparable figure.

### What actually changed, and what did not

The engine is the least changed part of the story. All three cars are the same
2.0-litre turbocharged four, and the MK8's extra 21 hp over the MK7 comes mostly
from calibration and turbo, not from a redesign. **Both MK8 generations make
exactly the same 241 hp and 273 lb-ft**, which is why the road-test gap between
them is weight and tires rather than power.

The gearbox is the change that actually alters the car. **The MK7.5's manual is
gone.** The 2015 car was a six-speed manual; every MK8 is a seven-speed
dual-clutch. Car and Driver's 2025 verdict is blunt about it: *"Despite missing a
core ingredient, the GTI remains a great hot hatch."*

The brakes did not change at all across the two MK8s — 13.4-inch vented front and
12.2-inch vented rear on both — so the seven-foot difference in stopping distance
is tire condition, pad compound and surface, not hardware.

The dimensions barely moved either. Wheelbase is 103.6 inches on the MK7 and the
2025, 103.4 on the 2022. The MK8.5 is actually **half an inch lower** than the
2022 at 57.7 inches against 58.2.

### The part nobody compares: which one you can legally tune

This is the table that decides it for a lot of buyers, and it has nothing to do
with lap times.

Both MK8 generations use the EA888 evo4 engine with a Simos 19 ECU. APR — one of
the largest VW tuners in the US, based in Opelika, Alabama — publishes the
California status of each calibration on the product page. Read on 16 November
2026:

| | APR Stage 1 | CARB status |
|---|---|---|
| **MK7** (EA888 Gen 3) | 290–337 hp, 320–375 lb-ft | **Has a CARB Executive Order** |
| **MK8/MK8.5** (EA888 Gen 4) | up to 328–478 hp | **NOT LEGAL FOR SALE OR USE IN CALIFORNIA** |

The MK8 page states it directly: *"This product is NOT LEGAL FOR SALE OR USE IN
THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA. Testing with the California Air Resources Board, in
order to achieve 50-state compliance, is pending with CARB."*

The MK7 upgrade, at **$502.95**, carries an EO and documents it — APR's own FAQ
answers *"We have a CARB EO on this tune"* to a customer asking whether it will
pass a California smog check. The MK8 upgrade is **$592.95** and cannot be sold
into California at all.

So the car with the better road-test scorecard is the one you cannot legally
tune in the largest car market in the country, and the older generation is the
one with a 337 hp street-legal calibration on file. If you live in California and
intend to tune, that single fact outweighs half a second to 60.

### APR's own notice kills Stage 2 for everyone

One line buried in APR's site footer applies regardless of which generation you
buy, and almost nobody reads it:

> *"Software tuning requiring or supporting the removal of factory-equipped
> emissions control systems (often referred to as 'Stage 2' for turbocharged
> vehicles) is not available for the US market."*

That is the tuner saying its Stage 2 is not sold in the US at all. It matches
APR's product page, which lists Stage 1, APR Plus, Stage 2, Stage 3 IS38, and
Stage 3 DTR6054 in the navigation — but the footer notice is the one that tells
you what can actually be shipped.

The practical consequence: **the traditional "downpipe plus Stage 2" path is not
a route a US tuner will sell you.** The downpipe you can buy; the calibration
that goes with it is the part that has been withdrawn. Budget a Stage 1 and stop,
or accept that the next step is hardware rather than software.

### What a downpipe alone is worth

The MK7's catted downpipe is the most-reviewed power part in the Golf's
aftermarket — MAP's **$499.99** catted downpipe carries **106 reviews** on
MAPerformance, read 16 November 2026. That volume says people buy it, and it is
the correct first hardware step on a turbo car because the factory downpipe is
the restriction behind the turbo, not the cat-back.

Read MAP's own listing for the emissions position and you find this, verbatim:
*"This direct replacement catted version helps eliminate some of the harmful
emissions so you don't have to worry about legalities or excessive pollution
(check your local and state laws to be sure)"* and a second line: *"Sound level
and emissions laws vary based on location. Please consult your local authorities."*

That is not a CARB claim, and it should not be read as one. A catted downpipe
that keeps a high-flow converter is a different legal object from a catless one,
but neither is automatically California-legal, and the listing does not assert an
EO number anywhere on the page. If you are in California, verify the EO on the
part before you buy it.

### The intake question, and the same trap

The MK8 APR intake (**$358.95**, list $399.95) is the natural first bolt-on, and
its page carries the identical warning: *"Testing with the California Air
Resources Board, in order to achieve 50-state compliance, is pending with CARB.
Once testing is complete with California, and an E.O. number is issued, this
product will be updated to 50-state legal status."*

So on the MK8 you have an intake and a tune that are both pending CARB, and on
the MK7 you have a tune with an EO on file. That asymmetry is the single most
useful thing in this article for a California buyer, and it is invisible in every
0–60 comparison on the internet.

### So which generation should you buy

**If you want the fastest car as delivered**, the 2022–2024 MK8 is the one. It
has the best road-test numbers of the three and it is eight tenths off the MK8.5
around VIR rather than ahead of it, which means the two are effectively tied on
track while the 2022 wins every single-number test.

**If you intend to tune and you live in California**, the MK7 is the car with a
legal 337 hp calibration available today. That is a real, documented advantage,
not a hypothetical one, and it is worth more than the MK7's older chassis.

**If you want a manual**, only the MK7 and MK7.5 have one. That decides it for
some people before any of the numbers above get read.

**If you are buying the 2025 expecting the best of everything**, budget for what
it actually is: the same engine as the 2022, more weight, the same brakes, and a
quicker lap. It is a good car. It is not a faster one on paper, and the paper is
worth reading before you pay for the newest model year.

The one thing that does not change across any of them is where the money goes
first. On a turbo four, the downpipe and the calibration are the power. The
cat-back is the sound. Buying them in the other order is the most common way GTI
owners spend twice.

### 다운파이프만으로 얻는 것

### 결론

**순정 상태로 가장 빠른 차**를 원하면 2022–2024 MK8입니다. 세 대 중 도로 시험

**수동이 필요하면** MK7과 MK7.5뿐입니다.

터보 4기통에서 출력은 다운파이프와 캘리브레이션이 만들고, 캣백은 소리를 만듭니다.
순서를 바꿔 사는 것이 GTI 오너가 돈을 두 번 쓰는 가장 흔한 방식입니다.

| 0–60 mph | 5,8 s | **5,1 s** | 5,6 s |
| Frenada 70–0 | 163 ft | **151 ft** | 158 ft |
| Skidpad | 0,91 g | **0,97 g** | 0,91 g |

Frente al de 2022, el de 2025 es **medio segundo más lento hasta 60 mph**, frena
**siete pies más tarde desde 70 mph** y pierde **0,06 g** de agarre, con la misma
potencia, el mismo par y los mismos frenos de 13,4 pulgadas delante y 12,2 detrás.
Además pesa **139 lb más**.

Dos matices honestos: la prueba de 2015 no lleva la nota *"omit 1-ft rollout of 0.3
sec"* que sí aparece en las de 2022 y 2025, así que su cifra no es necesariamente
comparable; y aquel coche era un **Performance package** de 220 hp. El MK7 estándar
era más lento, no más rápido.

### El circuito dice lo contrario

Car and Driver corre el mismo coche en Virginia International Raceway cada año. Los
dos MK8:

| | 2022 MK8 | 2025 MK8.5 |
|---|---|---|
| Vuelta | 3:12.1 | **3:11.3** |
| Peso en pista | 3311 lb | 3285 lb |
| Neumáticos | Hankook Ventus S1 evo3 | Hankook Ventus S1 evo3 |

**El de 2025 fue ocho décimas más rápido con el mismo modelo de neumático**, pese a
peor skidpad y peor frenada. El skidpad es un círculo en régimen constante y la
frenada un número de un día; una vuelta es el promedio de cuatro millas.

Eso significa que las dos generaciones están **lo bastante cerca como para que un
solo día de pruebas decida el resultado.** Elegir entre ambas por una décima es
elegir por ruido.

### Lo que cambió de verdad

El motor es lo menos cambiado. Los tres son el mismo cuatro cilindros turbo de 2,0
litros, y los 21 hp extra del MK8 vienen sobre todo de calibración y turbo. **Los dos
MK8 tienen exactamente la misma potencia y el mismo par**, así que su diferencia es
peso y neumáticos.

La caja sí cambia el coche: **el manual del MK7.5 desapareció.** El de 2015 era
manual de seis marchas; todos los MK8 son DCT de siete.

Los frenos no cambiaron entre los dos MK8, así que los siete pies salen de neumático,
compuesto de pastilla y superficie.

### La parte que nadie compara: cuál se puede reprogramar legalmente

Los dos MK8 usan el EA888 evo4 con ECU Simos 19. APR publica el estado en California
de cada calibración. Leído el 16 de noviembre de 2026:

| | APR Stage 1 | Estado CARB |
|---|---|---|
| **MK7** (EA888 Gen 3) | 290–337 hp, 320–375 lb-ft | **Tiene una Orden Ejecutiva CARB** |
| **MK8/MK8.5** (EA888 Gen 4) | hasta 328–478 hp | **NO LEGAL PARA VENTA O USO EN CALIFORNIA** |

La página del MK8 lo dice sin rodeos: *"This product is NOT LEGAL FOR SALE OR USE IN
THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA."* La del MK7, **$502.95**, documenta su EO y su FAQ
responde a un cliente que pregunta por la inspección de emisiones: *"We have a CARB
EO on this tune."* La del MK8 cuesta **$592.95** y no se puede vender en California.

Para quien viva en California y quiera reprogramar, ese dato pesa más que medio
segundo hasta 60 mph.

### El aviso de APR elimina el Stage 2 para todos

Una línea del pie de página de APR se aplica a cualquier generación:

> *"Software tuning requiring or supporting the removal of factory-equipped
> emissions control systems (often referred to as 'Stage 2' for turbocharged
> vehicles) is not available for the US market."*

Es el propio preparador diciendo que su Stage 2 no se vende en Estados Unidos. La
ruta clásica de **downpipe más Stage 2 no es una ruta que un preparador
estadounidense te venda**: el downpipe se compra, la calibración no existe.

### Conclusión

**El más rápido de serie** es el MK8 de 2022–2024. **Si vas a reprogramar en
California**, el MK7 es el que tiene hoy una calibración legal de 337 hp. **Si
quieres manual**, solo MK7 y MK7.5. En un turbo de cuatro cilindros la potencia sale
del downpipe y la calibración; el cat-back da el sonido. Comprarlos al revés es la
forma más común de que un propietario de GTI pague dos veces.
