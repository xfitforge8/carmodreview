# Offset and Backspacing: One Number Describes the Other

## EN

**Offset and backspacing describe the same thing from two different starting
points, which means a listing that publishes both can be checked against
itself.** Most shoppers treat them as two specs to memorize. They are one spec
stated twice, and the arithmetic connecting them is simple enough to do on a
phone in the aisle.

Every figure below was read from the product spec block on **18 November 2026**.

### The formula

**Backspacing = (wheel width + 1) ÷ 2 + (offset ÷ 25.4)**

Width is in inches, offset is in millimetres, and the answer is in inches. The
`+ 1` is the bead flange — the lip that holds the tire on — which is about half
an inch on each side, so it adds a full inch to the width you measure between.

Here it is against five wheels, with the arithmetic shown:

| Wheel | Width | Offset | Calculated | Listed backspace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vision SW60 | 6.50 in. | +40 mm | 5.32 in. | **5.33 in.** |
| Method MR305 | 8.50 in. | +25 mm | 5.73 in. | **5.70 in.** |
| Black Rhino Tembo | 9.00 in. | +12 mm | 5.47 in. | **5.47 in.** |
| Moto Metal MO970 | 9.00 in. | +18 mm | 5.71 in. | **5.71 in.** |
| Fuel Vapor D560 | 9.00 in. | +20 mm | 5.79 in. | **5.75 in.** |

Worst case is 0.04 in. — a millimetre. The relationship holds.

### What each one actually measures

**Offset** is the distance from the wheel's centre line to its mounting face.
Positive offset means the mounting face is outboard of centre, so the wheel sits
further in toward the truck. Negative offset puts the mounting face inboard of
centre and pushes the wheel out. It is measured in millimetres because that is
the unit the industry settled on.

**Backspacing** is the distance from the inner edge of the wheel to the mounting
face. It is measured in inches, from a hard edge you can put a straightedge
against, which is why it is the number that answers "will this hit my
suspension."

Offset's zero point is the wheel's centre line, which moves when the width
changes. Backspacing's zero point is the wheel's inner lip, which is a physical
edge. That difference is why the same backspace can appear on wheels with very
different offsets.

### The counterintuitive part

The **Vision SW60 is 6.5 inches wide with a +40 mm offset**, and its backspace is
**5.33 in.**

The **Black Rhino Tembo is 9 inches wide with a +12 mm offset**, and its
backspace is **5.47 in.**

The Vision has more than three times the positive offset, and the shallower
backspace. A +40 mm offset sounds like it pulls the wheel deep into the
wheelwell. On a narrow wheel, it does not — half the width is only 3.25 in., so
even 40 mm (1.57 in.) of offset only brings the back edge to 5.33 in.

Widen the wheel by 2.5 inches and the centre line moves outward by 1.25 in. all
by itself, which eats most of the difference before offset is even considered.

**So: a big positive offset number does not mean a wheel sits further in.** The
width moves the zero point, and width is the bigger lever of the two.

### Why backspacing is the number to check first

Clearance problems are almost always at the **inside** of the wheel — the upper
control arm, the steering knuckle, the inner fender, the brake caliper. That is
exactly what backspacing measures, directly, with no conversion.

If you know your current wheel's backspace and you want the new one to sit the
same way relative to the suspension, match that number. If you are changing
width, the offset has to move to keep backspace constant — and that is what the
formula tells you.

Two worked examples from the table:

- Going from the **9-inch Tembo at +12 mm** (5.47 in.) to a wider or narrower
  wheel at the same backspace requires recalculating the offset, not copying it.
- The **Fuel D560 at 9 inches and +20 mm** has 0.28 in. more backspace than the
  Tembo at 9 inches and +12 mm. Same width, so the whole difference is the
  8 mm of offset — which is 0.31 in., and the 0.03 in. gap is rounding in the
  published figures.

### The other numbers on the same spec row

Four more figures sit beside offset and backspacing, and two of them decide
whether the wheel goes on at all:

**Bolt pattern.** Read as a count and a circle diameter. The wheels here run
`6 x 135mm`, `6 x 139.70mm`, `8 x 165.10mm` and `8 x 180mm` — all truck
patterns, none interchangeable. The listing shows both metric and inch forms
where they differ; `6 x 139.70mm` is the same as `6 x 5.5 in.`

**Center bore.** The hole in the middle that sits over the hub. These five
range from **57.10 mm** on the Vision to **125.10 mm** on the Moto Metal —
a 68 mm spread that no title or price reveals. A bore larger than your hub is
usually corrected with a hub-centric ring; a bore *smaller* than your hub means
the wheel will not seat at all.

**Load rating.** Published per wheel, and it varies more than people expect:
**1,520 lbs.** on the Vision steel wheel against **3,700 lbs.** on the Fuel
D560. On a truck that carries or tows, this is a specification rather than a
footnote.

**Wheel weight.** Given on some listings and not others. The Vision shows
**12.9 lbs.** and the Black Rhino **33.2 lbs.** — but those are a 16x6.5 steel
wheel and an 18x9 aluminum one, so the gap is size and material, not quality.

### Using the formula on a listing that looks wrong

This is the practical use, and it is the reason the relationship matters.

A listing that publishes width, offset and backspacing has published the same
information twice. Run the formula. If the calculated backspace is more than
about 0.1 in. off the listed figure, one of the three numbers on that page is
wrong, and you cannot tell which from the page. That is a listing to verify with
the manufacturer before you spend $400 on a wheel.

An exact match is not required — published figures are rounded, which is why
four of the five above sit within 0.04 in. rather than at zero. A number that is
out by half an inch is not rounding.

### What offset changes once the wheel is on

Offset does more than decide clearance, and this is the part most buyers meet
after the fact.

It sets the **scrub radius** — the distance between where the tire's contact
patch centre sits and where the steering axis meets the ground. Move the wheel
outboard with a lower offset and that distance grows. The practical results are
heavier steering effort at low speed, more kickback through the wheel over
bumps, and more load on the wheel bearings because the load is applied further
out along the hub.

None of that is visible in a photo of the truck sitting still, which is why
offset gets bought on looks and lived with afterwards.

### The lift interaction worth knowing

On a lifted truck, backspacing and clearance interact, because lifting changes
where the suspension sits through its travel. A wheel that clears at ride height
can contact an upper control arm at full droop.

The wheels above range from **5.33 in. to 5.79 in. of backspace**, and that
0.46 in. band is narrow in the context of a lift. If you are choosing wheels and
a lift together, the backspace is the figure to confirm against the lift
manufacturer's own clearance guidance rather than against the original wheel.

### Does any of this touch California emissions law?

No. Wheels are not emissions equipment, there is no CARB Executive Order to
check, and wheel specification has no bearing on a smog inspection. This is a
fitment and handling question.

### What I could not verify

- **Fitment on any specific vehicle.** The applications tables on these listings
  load separately and I read the specification blocks, not the application
  lists. Confirm by year, make, model and trim before ordering.
- **Whether the flange is exactly 1 inch on every wheel.** The formula uses
  `width + 1`, which matched these five wheels to within 0.04 in. Flange
  thickness varies slightly by construction, so treat a small gap as normal and
  a large one as a red flag rather than assuming the constant is exact.
- **The load rating basis.** The figures are quoted as published per wheel. I
  did not verify whether each manufacturer's rating accounts for dynamic load
  or a safety factor, and those are not comparable across brands without the
  manufacturer's own documentation.
- **Prices.** I read them but am not quoting them here, because this article is
  about specification and the prices move. The spec rows are the durable part.

## KR

**오프셋과 백스페이싱은 같은 것을 서로 다른 기준점에서 잰 값입니다.** 그래서 둘 다
적힌 상품 페이지는 스스로를 검산할 수 있습니다. 대부분 두 개의 스펙으로 외우지만,
실제로는 하나의 스펙을 두 번 적은 것이고, 연결하는 계산은 휴대폰으로도 됩니다.

모든 수치는 **2026년 11월 18일** 상품 스펙란에서 직접 확인했습니다.

**백스페이싱 = (휠 폭 + 1) ÷ 2 + (오프셋 ÷ 25.4)**

폭은 인치, 오프셋은 밀리미터, 결과는 인치입니다. `+1`은 타이어를 잡아주는 비드
플랜지로, 양쪽 각각 약 0.5인치입니다.

| 휠 | 폭 | 오프셋 | 계산값 | 표기 백스페이스 |

| Black Rhino Tembo | 9.00 in. | +12 mm | 5.47 in. | **5.47 in.** |
| Moto Metal MO970 | 9.00 in. | +18 mm | 5.71 in. | **5.71 in.** |
| Fuel Vapor D560 | 9.00 in. | +20 mm | 5.79 in. | **5.75 in.** |

최대 오차 0.04인치, 1밀리미터 수준입니다.

### 각각이 재는 것

**오프셋**은 휠 중심선에서 마운팅 면까지의 거리입니다. 양의 오프셋은 마운팅 면이
중심선보다 바깥쪽에 있어 휠이 차체 안쪽으로 들어갑니다. 밀리미터로 씁니다.

**백스페이싱**은 휠 안쪽 가장자리에서 마운팅 면까지입니다. 인치로 쓰고, 자를 댈 수
있는 물리적 모서리에서 재기 때문에 "서스펜션에 닿는가"에 직접 답합니다.

오프셋의 영점은 폭이 바뀌면 함께 움직이는 중심선이고, 백스페이싱의 영점은 고정된
안쪽 립입니다. 그래서 같은 백스페이스가 전혀 다른 오프셋에 나타납니다.

### 직관과 반대인 부분

**Vision SW60은 폭 6.5인치에 +40 mm**, 백스페이스 **5.33인치**입니다.
**Black Rhino Tembo는 폭 9인치에 +12 mm**, 백스페이스 **5.47인치**입니다.

Vision의 양의 오프셋이 세 배 이상인데 백스페이스는 더 얕습니다. 폭이 2.5인치
늘어나면 중심선만 1.25인치 바깥으로 이동하기 때문입니다.

**즉, 양의 오프셋 숫자가 크다고 휠이 더 안쪽으로 들어가는 것이 아닙니다.** 영점을
움직이는 것은 폭이고, 폭이 더 큰 변수입니다.

### 간섭은 안쪽에서 생깁니다

간섭은 거의 항상 휠 **안쪽**에서 발생합니다. 어퍼 컨트롤암, 너클, 이너 펜더,
캘리퍼입니다. 백스페이싱이 바로 그 값을 직접 잽니다. 순정 휠의 백스페이스를 알고
있다면 그 숫자를 맞추면 됩니다.

### 같은 스펙란의 다른 숫자들

**볼트 패턴**: `6 x 135mm`, `6 x 139.70mm`, `8 x 165.10mm`, `8 x 180mm`로 모두
호환되지 않습니다. `6 x 139.70mm`는 `6 x 5.5 in.`와 같습니다.

**센터 보어**: **57.10 mm**(Vision)에서 **125.10 mm**(Moto Metal)까지 68 mm
차이입니다. 허브보다 크면 허브링으로 보정하고, 작으면 장착 자체가 안 됩니다.

**하중 등급**: **1,520 lbs.**(Vision 스틸)에서 **3,700 lbs.**(Fuel D560)까지
차이가 큽니다. 적재나 견인이 있다면 각주가 아니라 스펙입니다.

### 의심스러운 페이지를 검산하는 법

폭·오프셋·백스페이스를 모두 적은 페이지는 같은 정보를 두 번 적은 것입니다. 공식을
돌려서 계산값이 표기값과 0.1인치 이상 차이 나면 그중 하나는 틀린 것이고, 페이지
만으로는 어느 것인지 알 수 없습니다. 제조사에 확인할 페이지입니다.

### 캘리포니아

휠은 배기가스 장치가 아니므로 CARB Executive Order 대상이 아니고 스모그 검사와
무관합니다. 장착과 주행 특성의 문제입니다.

## ES

**El offset y el backspacing describen lo mismo desde dos puntos de partida
distintos, así que una ficha que publica ambos puede comprobarse a sí misma.**
Todas las cifras se leyeron en el bloque de especificaciones el **18 de noviembre
de 2026**.

### La fórmula

**Backspacing = (ancho + 1) ÷ 2 + (offset ÷ 25.4)**

Ancho en pulgadas, offset en milímetros, resultado en pulgadas. El `+1` es la
pestaña del talón, aproximadamente media pulgada por lado.

| Llanta | Ancho | Offset | Calculado | Backspace publicado |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vision SW60 | 6,50 in. | +40 mm | 5,32 in. | **5,33 in.** |
| Method MR305 | 8,50 in. | +25 mm | 5,73 in. | **5,70 in.** |
| Black Rhino Tembo | 9,00 in. | +12 mm | 5,47 in. | **5,47 in.** |
| Moto Metal MO970 | 9,00 in. | +18 mm | 5,71 in. | **5,71 in.** |
| Fuel Vapor D560 | 9,00 in. | +20 mm | 5,79 in. | **5,75 in.** |

La diferencia máxima es 0,04 in.: un milímetro.

### Qué mide cada uno

El **offset** es la distancia desde la línea central de la llanta hasta la cara de
montaje, en milímetros. Su punto cero es la línea central, que se mueve al cambiar
el ancho.

El **backspacing** es la distancia desde el borde interior hasta la cara de
montaje, en pulgadas. Su punto cero es un borde físico, y por eso responde
directamente a "¿tocará la suspensión?".

### La parte contraintuitiva

La **Vision SW60 mide 6,5 in. de ancho con +40 mm** de offset y su backspace es
**5,33 in.** La **Black Rhino Tembo mide 9 in. con +12 mm** y su backspace es
**5,47 in.**

La Vision tiene más de tres veces el offset positivo y aun así el backspace más
somero. Un ancho mayor desplaza el punto cero 1,25 in. por sí solo.

**Un número de offset positivo grande no significa que la llanta quede más
adentro.** El ancho mueve el cero, y el ancho pesa más.

### Los demás números de la misma fila

**Patrón de pernos:** `6 x 135mm`, `6 x 139.70mm`, `8 x 165.10mm`, `8 x 180mm`;
ninguno es intercambiable.

**Buje central:** de **57,10 mm** a **125,10 mm**, una diferencia de 68 mm que
ningún título revela. Mayor que el cubo se corrige con un anillo; menor no monta.

**Capacidad de carga:** de **1.520 lbs.** a **3.700 lbs.** por rueda. En un
vehículo que carga o remolca es una especificación, no una nota al pie.

### Comprobar una ficha dudosa

Si una ficha da ancho, offset y backspace, ha dado la misma información dos veces.
Aplique la fórmula: una diferencia de más de 0,1 in. significa que uno de los tres
números está mal, y la página no dice cuál.

### California

Las llantas no son equipo de emisiones: no hay Executive Order de CARB y no
afectan a la inspección de humos.
