# Bentley Will Sell You a Better Screen. It Costs Almost Every Button.

## EN

Bentley has announced an infotainment retrofit for second-generation Continental
GT and Flying Spur models that replaces the original touchscreen with a larger,
faster unit. The upgrade deletes most of the physical climate controls below the
screen to fit it.

Bentley announced the programme in the week of November 13, 2026, for an
**undisclosed price**.

### What you get

The new head unit is a **9.7-inch touchscreen** at **2K (1440p) resolution**, with
Bentley claiming "impressively fast processor speeds" and "the latest navigation
mapping." Critically for cars of this age, it supports both **wired and wireless
phone projection** — meaning Apple CarPlay, which Bentley did not fit to these
cars at the factory.

That is the real argument for the retrofit. A second-generation Continental GT or
Flying Spur predates CarPlay in a Bentley, and adding it to an otherwise
well-built interior is a legitimate want, not vanity.

### What it costs you in hardware

The original dash put a small screen above a bank of switches, dials and a
dedicated climate readout. Fitting a 9.7-inch display means those go.

Most climate functions move into the touchscreen. **The CD player is removed.**
Some controls survive: **A/C, front and rear defrost, the navigation shortcut,
and what appears to be a voice-control trigger** remain as physical inputs. Seat
heating and ventilation also stay, but only because their controls were already
located south of the shifter rather than below the screen.

Exactly **two knobs** carry over.

So the trade is precise and worth stating plainly: you gain CarPlay, a modern
screen and current navigation. You lose the ability to change cabin temperature
without looking at a screen.

### Why this is a genuinely strange product

The retrofit arrives at a moment when the industry is moving the other way. It is
the same week Audi's US product chief told The Drive that Audi had gone as far as
it would on touchscreens and was heading back toward mechanical controls.

Bentley is therefore selling, as an upgrade, exactly the design decision that
other manufacturers are now backing away from. That does not make it wrong for a
Bentley owner — these are ten-year-old cars, and factory support for a decade-old
infotainment system is rare and worth something. But an owner should go in
knowing they are buying a 2018 answer in 2026.

### An OEM retrofit is a different product from an aftermarket one

It is worth being clear about what is actually being sold here, because the
aftermarket already solved "old Bentley, no CarPlay" years ago.

An aftermarket head unit is cheaper and often more feature-dense. What it does
not have is the things a factory programme brings: integration with the car's own
menu structure, steering wheel and cluster inputs wired to work the way the car
was designed, and a part that a dealer will stand behind. On a car where the
climate panel, the seat controls and the infotainment are all one system, ripping
out the factory screen and dropping in a third-party unit frequently costs
function elsewhere — and leaves the car's interior looking modified rather than
optioned.

That is the real case for paying Bentley rather than an installer. It is the same
reason a factory tow package outsells a better aftermarket hitch.

### The two-knob question

Of everything in the announcement, "exactly two knobs carry over" is the detail
that should drive the decision, and it is under-specified in the material
released.

Two knobs out of a full bank is a large reduction. Which two survive determines
how usable the car is on the move — a volume knob is nearly essential, and if the
second is something like a drive-mode selector rather than cabin temperature, then
every temperature change after the retrofit requires a glance away from the road.

Ask to sit in a converted car before committing. Dealers arranging retrofits
often have a completed example, and five minutes in one answers this better than
any specification sheet.

### What the retrofit does not change

Nothing in the announcement suggests the upgrade touches the car's cameras,
driver assistance systems, or audio amplification. It is a head unit and a trim
panel. If the reason you are considering it is that the reversing camera looks
dated, this is not the fix.

### The question to ask before booking

The price is not published, and that matters more than usual here. This is a
dealer-installed retrofit on a low-volume luxury car. Historically, retrofit
programmes of this kind price in the thousands, but **we have no figure and will
not guess at one.**

Ask the dealer three things before committing: the total installed cost, which two
knobs survive, and whether the original climate panel can be retained or is
discarded permanently — because if it is discarded, the retrofit is difficult to
reverse, and the car's original specification is gone.

### What we could not verify

The price, in any form. Bentley has not disclosed it and no dealer pricing has
been published.

We also could not verify:
- Which specific model years within the second generation are eligible
- Whether the retrofit is available in every market or US-only
- Whether the removed climate panel is retained, or whether the conversion can be
  reversed
- Any independent measurement of the new unit's performance beyond Bentley's own
  claims
