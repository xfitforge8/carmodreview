# Full-Size Trucks 0–60 by Trim: F-150, Silverado, Sierra, Ram 1500, Tundra

## EN

The quickest half-ton for 2025 or 2026 does not have a V-8 in it.

Car and Driver recorded **4.2 seconds** to 60 mph in a 2025 Ram 1500 Limited 4x4
Crew Cab with the 540-hp Hurricane twin-turbo inline-six. That beats every figure
the outlet has recorded for a 6.2-liter Silverado, a 6.2-liter Sierra, a
5.0-liter F-150 or a twin-turbo Tundra.

The caveat that matters: most of these tests were not run on a 2025 or 2026
truck. The Silverado's 5.4-second run dates to 2018, the Sierra's to 2022, the
Tundra Limited's to 2022. Same generation, older test — so every figure below
carries its test year and outlet.

### Ram 1500

| Trim | Engine | 0–60 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limited 4x4 Crew | 540-hp 3.0L Hurricane I-6 | **4.2 sec** | [Car and Driver, 2024](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a62748761/2025-ram-1500-limited-crew-cab-4x4-test/) |
| Rebel | 420-hp 3.0L Hurricane I-6 | **4.9 sec** | [Car and Driver, 2024](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/comparison-test/a62762894/2024-ford-f-150-tremor-2025-ram-1500-rebel/) |
| Hemi V-8 | 5.7L V-8 (returning for 2026) | **6.0 sec** | [Car and Driver, 2025](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a65958660/2026-ram-1500-hemi-drive/) |
| Base V-6 | 3.6L V-6 | **no published test found** | — |

The Hurricane replaced the Hemi and beat it by 2.2 seconds. Car and Driver's
account: the 2025 Limited *"laid waste to a '22 with the 5.7-liter V-8 eTorque we
tested previously… hitting 60 mph in 4.2 seconds, 2.2 ticks sooner than its Hemi
predecessor and just 0.5 second off the 702-hp Ram TRX (RIP)."*

The Hemi came back for 2026 and ran 6.0 — level with the 5.0-liter F-150 Tremor
and 1.8 seconds behind the Hurricane in the same truck. Car and Driver titled its
follow-up ["Ram's Hurricane Turbo Six Storms Past the Hemi V-8 in Our
Testing"](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a69472310/ram-1500-turbo-six-cylinder-quicker-than-hemi-v8-test/).

The hybrid is the quick one. The two Tremor figures are the interesting pair: the
2021 truck with the twin-turbo V-6 beat the 2024 truck with the V-8 by 0.7
second, which is why the V-6 stayed the enthusiast pick. Car and Driver lists the
same three numbers on its 2025 and 2026 F-150 pages.

Every Silverado figure Car and Driver publishes traces to an older test — the 5.4
to a December 2018 article whose results block reads *"Zero to 60 mph: 5.4 sec,"*
the 6.1 to a November 2018 one. Both are on the current 2025 and 2026 model
pages, which is how a 2018 number reads as current if you only see the page.

The diesel needs care. The 277-hp LM2 was replaced by the 305-hp LZ0 for 2023,
and the LM2's numbers are still quoted as if they describe the current truck. Car
and Driver's Sierra page carries a **6.8-second** diesel figure without saying
which engine — it matches neither published LM2 run (7.0 MotorTrend, 7.1 C/D).
For the LZ0, MotorTrend wrote only that the revised engine *"is probably quicker
now"* — a prediction, not a test. No instrumented LZ0 figure turned up, so the
row says so.

Toyota's tested trims span 0.6 second, from 5.5 to 6.1 — worth knowing before you
assume the trim you can afford gets the quick version. The Limited's test called
its 6.1 *"a modest 6.1 seconds, putting it 0.4 second behind the TRD Pro,"* and
the arithmetic holds: 5.7 plus 0.4 is 6.1. The 5.6-second TRD Performance Package
figure is the only current-model-year Tundra result here.

### The class, in one line

The four quickest configurations are two inline-sixes, a hybrid V-6 and a
twin-turbo V-6; the 6.2-liter V-8s are fifth and sixth. From the Ram's 4.2 to the
sea-level Silverado 5.3's 6.1 is 1.9 seconds — a gap you feel on every on-ramp.
And only three of these figures come from a 2025-or-later truck.

## Why the numbers vary

**Car and Driver omits a 1-ft rollout.** Every C/D result above carries the
footnote *"Results above omit 1-ft rollout of 0.3 sec."* That makes its figures
about **0.3 second quicker** than a standing-start measurement of the same truck,
and means they are not directly comparable to a manufacturer's claim. Applied
consistently, so C/D figures are comparable to each other.

**TFLtruck tests at about a mile of altitude.** From Colorado: *"We clocked our
new Trail Boss 0-60 MPH at 9.46 seconds at a mile above sea level with three
people in the truck."* On the same route and day a 6.2-liter Silverado RST ran
**7.70 seconds** against the 5.3 Trail Boss's **9.46** — a 1.76-second gap, where
at sea level Car and Driver finds 0.7 second between those engines. Thin air
penalizes naturally aspirated engines unevenly. **Do not compare that 9.46 with
the sea-level figures without saying which is which.**

**Manufacturer claims are a different category.** Ram quoted 0–60 in **4.4
seconds** for the Ramcharger, the extended-range electric 1500. Road & Track
reported Ram's *final* figures on 25 February 2025 as **647 hp and 4.5 seconds**,
attributed to the manufacturer. Both are claims, not instrumented tests, and
neither is ranked against one.
